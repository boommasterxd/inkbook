# History Replay Reuse

Status: Accepted.

## Context

Undo of a deletion previously rebuilt Store records, copied stroke samples, recalculated bounds, and discarded previously prepared scene contours. These costs remain material for the 10,000-stroke document even when preparation yields under the central scheduler.

## Decision

Each Store privately associates an applied mutation with its original and replacement records. Only changed records are retained. Public transition data remains a separate input boundary: replay validates it again and compares it exactly with a private record before reusing that record's immutable element and bounds. Mutated samples, foreign transitions, changed shape fields, payload overflow, and stale preparation retain their normal rejection or reconstruction behavior. No public flag grants trusted replay.

Internal document, clipboard, and eraser history share one replay adapter. It selects the Store's explicit retained-history entry point, which requires private transition provenance and verifies current source record identities before preparing canonical destinations. Public transition element copies are not read by this entry point. Retained samples, headers, and bounds therefore need no repeated staging or equality traversal. Directional orders remain frozen, connected endpoints and resulting membership are checked, and capacity, index, cancellation, and atomic publication retain their normal preparation. Only an exact canonical resulting membership can reuse the privately validated history payload bound. Foreign transitions and changed source records cannot enter this path. The public mutation/replay API retains complete validation and supports changed public samples.

Eligible restoration creates current commit-time version wrappers around existing owned samples and bounds. Capacity, connections, spatial-index preparation, cancellation, and atomic publication retain their existing paths. Both synchronous removal registration and prepared mutation commits use the same private ownership mechanism. Additional private record and direction-map ownership counts conservatively toward the existing history payload limit; it does not create an unbounded cache. Weak transition ownership permits history eviction and disposal to release the retained records.

Specialized synchronous and cooperative replacement replay uses the same record-restoration producer. A Store-private weak identity registry resolves validated immutable record elements; preparation carries the retained record through private staging and connection resolution. Successful replacement commits supply canonical before and after elements, including propagated connections, to the existing history. Capturing this data replaces the original transform-input references. The shared retained-record accounting covers these commands under the existing history cap. Local replacement replay keeps its specialized index patches and preview handoff without constructing complete band orders.

Structural membership changes publish only affected records when the surviving elements preserve their relative band order. Their delta domains are classified directly after the shared admission checkpoint, without unused rank lookups or a second cooperative producer. Exact dense ranks still update; shifted ranks alone do not create per-survivor changes. Replacements, true arrange commands, and unproved reorders retain their complete classification and order notifications. Every changed band remains dirty, including removal of its last element, so worker persistence receives the complete resulting order.

Arrange availability reads selected ranks and constant-time Store band sizes instead of materializing complete band orders. Selected Style changes, Arrange, selected removal, and whole-document removal prepare under the shared interactive frame admission in the active editor. Prepared Style changes use the same local replacement history as transforms and retain every propagated connection replacement; replay reuses private before and after records, including bounds after a width change. Style changes do not collect complete band orders. Selection frame effects remain tied to successful atomic commits.

An exact Store-owned history direction may reuse its frozen order validation only at the privately recorded mutation version and after its resulting membership matches the privately retained records. An unchanged authoritative band can retain its current mutable array. Changed bands still prepare copied orders and dense rank patches under the scheduler; history arrays never become mutable Store arrays. Foreign transitions, changed public data, and intervening Store mutations fall back to complete validation.

Private canonical replay proves resulting membership during the existing connection-resolution pass, including unexpected detachments, before publication. It does not stage another explicit-element array or repeat the membership scan; public replay keeps its complete membership check. Delta production streams changed IDs and otherwise necessary order ranks without a temporary affected-ID set. Skipped ranks retain scheduler admission slots. Capacity projects before and after records lazily from the prepared delta, preserving complete removal-before-addition phases, fanout limits, cancellation, patch generations, and atomic consumption instead of staging two record arrays.

Connection detachment preparation skips the removal walk when the authoritative reverse connection map is empty. With a nonempty map, referenceless targets skip helper allocation after the shared checkpoint. Referenced targets keep the complete detachment path; subsequent record preparation retains removal-ID validation, cancellation, stale-version checks, and atomic publication.

The existing derived-state delta pass also proves whether either capacity direction has any changed records. Entirely empty directions use an empty iterable instead of visiting absent-record slots. Nonempty directions keep their lazy complete projection, including null slots in mixed changes, removal-before-insertion order, actual record validation, fanout limits, cancellation, and patch generations.

Derived-state preparation reuses that connection proof only for exact immutable destinations supplied by private canonical replay. Collection has already checked those elements against the same resulting membership; creating current version wrappers does not change their endpoints. Public replay copies and dependent elements without that proof retain connection validation. Non-connectable records need no proof lookup. This reuse changes neither cancellation nor the Store-version and commit checks.

Settled viewport candidate ownership can patch known local insertions and removals directly. Unknown bounds, an unfinished spatial acquisition, or stale ownership retain the normal invalidation path. Numeric rank shifts permit an entrant-only merge when freshly classified ranks prove that the retained visible sequence remains ordered. Actual reorders still require complete sorting. This removes fresh broadphase acquisition and full sorting for the proved local case; it does not remove exact candidate classification or suffix rank maintenance.

The scene removes deleted ID/version aliases immediately. Immutable canonical stroke contours may remain retired within the existing path LRU, including retained sample and bookkeeping bytes. Reuse requires the same eligible immutable sample identity and canonical shape fields; offset placement remains separate from local contour identity. A new committed geometry version never authorizes a stale active alias. Mutable external arrays and incompatible geometry remain cache misses. No removed DOM node is retained for this reuse.

Retained contours may memoize signatures for privately marked, fully immutable stroke headers. Each retained contour owns at most one weak header memo; changing its owner, eviction, disabled retention, and disposal remove that memo. Additional token and weak-reference accounting remains within the existing contour byte limit. Availability probes do not promote aliases. The immediately preceding contour access can hand off its existing alias and LRU promotion to mount retention; an intervening access or invalidation clears that opportunity. Remote preparation publishes pressure paths and adopts canonical contours once before mount, using the same path for both retention policies. Public mutable headers continue to recompute their signatures.

Canonical geometry updates of already mounted elements use the same contour retention boundary before publication. Progressive preparation and Store-prepared paths retain an eligible contour only when its current ID/version alias is absent; warm aliases avoid duplicate promotion. Speculative live previews never pass through this canonical handoff. Deleting an edited stroke can therefore invalidate Store pressure paths while the bounded scene contour remains reusable for history restoration.

Optional removal visuals can use existing mounted canonical path parts instead of reconstructing their geometry. The visual still owns its own bounded nodes and preserves the stroke's local-to-world placement.

Worker model synchronization applies validated element facts and invalidates changed export fragments without generating their SVG, metadata, or sample encoding. Export and explicit prewarming resolve those fragments through the existing serializer and bounded caches. Successful removal may retire compact sample encodings inside the existing encoding LRU when current exclusive ownership of typed samples is proved before removal. Restoration reuses only the exact immutable sample identity after current model ownership is established. Different buffers, mutable inputs, model resets, eviction, and disposal remove this opportunity. Unchanged exclusively owned sample buffers remain reusable during model adoption. Revision-bound prepared pressure paths are adopted before lazy export resolution, without a separate deferred-fragment implementation. Native workers and the main-thread fallback share this behavior.

Eviction, memory pressure, disabled retention, and disposal release aliases, identity lookups, and contour ownership through the existing cache lifecycle. Main-thread and worker ownership remain independent. Cooperative preparation continues to use the injected central `WorkScheduler`; reuse introduces no scheduling loop or deadline.

## Verification

Deterministic tests observe sample/bounds identity, record construction, canonical path builds, cache bytes, aliases, mutation rejection, cancellation, and eviction. Capacity scenarios use the complete 10,000-stroke fixture. Correct restoration and bounded work are distinct assertions; passing them does not establish physical-device latency.

Public mutation replay still validates and exactly compares public transition samples. Internal retained-history replay uses privately owned values instead. The canonical resource limits remain in [Rendering and Performance](../rendering-and-performance.md) and history behavior remains in [Features](../features.md#undo-and-redo).
